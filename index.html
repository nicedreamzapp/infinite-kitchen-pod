The mission
Bring the cost of food down for people who like to eat out.

A big share of a restaurant bill pays for the kitchen around the food: rent, buildout and staff. The pod shrinks all three. What it keeps spending on is the ingredients.

Fig 01 / Ingredients
Quality ingredients

Cheaper because it's automated, not because it's worse.

The savings come out of rent, buildout and labor, never the food. A refrigerated zone keeps fresh ingredients at 34 to 38°F, and every portion is weighed.

  • Fresh, restocked daily. A person loads standard ingredient cartridges and sauce lines once a day.
  • Weighed to half a gram. Load-cell dispensers put the full portion in every bowl, every time.
  • 28% of the ticket. Food stays the biggest line item the model spends on.
Fig 02 / Cooking
Cooking

Real wok heat, two pans at once.

A 6-axis collaborative arm rides a linear track between two 5 kW induction woks and a ventless multi-cook oven, interleaving meals so neither pan sits idle.

  • 600°F wok sear in seconds, with a tossing motion for real wok hei.
  • Four modes: wok, steam and bake, chilled bowls, and sealed packing.
  • About 38 seconds a meal on paper. Proving it on a prototype is open issue 2.
Fig 03 / Self-cleaning
Self-cleaning

It washes itself after every single meal.

No dish pit, no closing shift. A 3-stage wash runs between orders, and a full clean-in-place cycle sprays down the whole cell off-peak.

  • Hydro-blast: a 60 PSI hot water jet clears the bulk.
  • Ultrasonic dip: 40 kHz cavitation with a food-safe sanitizer.
  • 180°F rinse: a final hot rinse aimed at a 5-log pathogen reduction.
  • Sloped 316L basin with a 2 inch air gap to the drain.

Fig 05 / Delivery
Delivery

Sealed, labeled and on its way.

Every meal is heat-sealed and labeled, then handed off without anyone touching it.

  • Vehicle bay: a side pass-through drops meals into a delivery vehicle.
  • Rooftop pad: an internal lift carries sealed meals up for drone dispatch.
  • Ground robots: an airlock conveyor hands off to delivery robots.
Fig 06 / Exterior
The footprint

120 square feet. All electric.

One pod is 12 ft by 10 ft and 9.5 ft tall, about 120 square feet. It runs on 480V 3-phase power with no gas line, and its own ventless hood means no ductwork and no restaurant lease.

  • Self-contained: power, water and drain in, meals out. No gas hookup.
  • Stainless frame with insulated, washable panels.
  • Some older docs still list other sizes and power; help bring them in line.
Fig 07 / Lockers
Pickup

Scan, open, eat.

Walk up, scan a code and your meal is waiting at the right temperature. No line, no counter, no tip screen.

  • Dual-climate lockers: 140°F hot and 38°F cold.
  • Mobile QR pickup straight from the order.
How it compares

Others automate a station. This is the whole kitchen, in the open.

Companies are automating kitchens with closed designs, and most of them automate one station inside a kitchen someone already built. As far as we can find, nobody has published an open, end-to-end design like this one. Fewer rented robots and less buildout means lower menu prices with the same good ingredients.

Two ways in

Deploy as a pod, or drop it into an existing kitchen.

Either way, money not spent on buildout and rent is money that never has to be charged for the meal.

Second-generation kitchen
$67k to $238k saved in buildout (estimate)

A gutted restaurant space that still has its hood, fire suppression, grease trap and plumbing. The cooking cell drops in and reuses all of it, skipping most of the buildout. The tradeoff is a lease and an older hood that may need bringing up to code.

Self-contained pod
No ductwork no walls, no lease

Its own UL 710B ventless hood and clean-in-place system let it sit on a sidewalk, in a parking lot or any small, low-rent footprint with power, water and a drain.

Buildout savings are general estimates, not quotes. Details in docs/retrofit-existing-kitchen.md.

Two ways to get one

Buy it outright, or run it as a service.

Two plans in the brochure, both early estimates. Either way the operator's costs stay low, so the menu prices can too.

Plan 1 / Turnkey purchase
$185,000 per pod, upfront

Own the hardware. A $1,200 a month cloud platform fee, a 4.5 month simple payback in the brochure model, and the most cash flow over the long run.

Plan 2 / Robot as a service
$8,500 a month, plus $15,000 setup

Low upfront cost. Hardware, software, telemetry, preventive maintenance and replacement parts are all in the subscription, which suits fast fleet growth.

Plan details are on the last page of the investor brochure.

Status: early concept, help wanted

Here's what isn't solved yet.

No prototype exists yet, and these are real open problems. Each one is an open issue. If you know the field, jump in.

Hot water buffer sizing

Each full clean-in-place run needs about 142 gallons of 180°F water. The tank holds 75.

Single-arm, dual-wok throughput

38 seconds a meal works on paper. It needs a prototype to prove it.

BOM and the $185k price

Early estimates from list prices. Real quotes wanted.

Hood and fire suppression

Integrating the UL 710B ventless hood and UL 300 suppression with the robot cell.

Material choices

Induction wok metal, peristaltic tubing, and the arm's washdown rating.

Allergen carryover

The wash protocol needs real carryover testing between meals.

Certification path

NSF 4/7, UL 197/471/508A, R15.06 / ISO 10218.

Reconcile the numbers

Footprint, amperage and finance figures differ across the docs.
